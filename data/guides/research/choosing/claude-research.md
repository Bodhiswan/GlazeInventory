# Choosing a Glaze — Claude research

Research for `/guides/glazing-pottery/choosing-a-glaze` ("How to Choose a Glaze").
Single-model research (Claude only); GPT and Grok have not reviewed this section, so there
is no arbitration entry. Every source below was opened and read on 2026-11-22. Quotes from
PDFs were read directly; wording from HTML pages came through a text extraction and is
paraphrased in the live guide rather than quoted.

Scope: commercial (jarred) glazes, mainly cone 5–6, for hobby and studio potters.

---

### Cone 6 and cone 06 are different firings
**Statement:** Orton self-supporting cones heated at 108°F/hr (60°C/hr) over the last 180°F (100°C): cone 06 = 1828°F / 998°C, cone 6 = 2232°F / 1222°C, cone 10 = 2345°F.
**Source:** Orton Ceramic Foundation, *Temperature Equivalents for Orton Pyrometric Cones (°F)* (2016), https://www.theceramicshop.com/technical_info/Cones/Orton-Cone-Chart-F-022-14.pdf ; Orton, "When is a Cone 6 a Cone 6?", https://www.ortonceramic.com/post/when-is-a-cone-6-a-cone-6
**Confidence:** High
**Verification:** Chart PDF and Orton post opened. The ortonceramic.com copy of the chart PDF returned 404, so the retailer-hosted copy is cited.
**Notes:** Orton's post gives °C for cone 06 and cone 6 only. The cone 10 Celsius figure (1285°C) is from Mayco's stoneware guide, which lists "CONE 10 (high fire) 1285°C / 2345°F".

### Match the glaze to the clay body's firing
**Statement:** Follow the firing recommendations of your clay body; small differences in how a kiln fires change how tough and sealed the surface becomes.
**Source:** Mayco, "Dinnerware Suitability and ACMI Information", https://www.maycocolors.com/resources/dinnerware-food-safety/ ; AMACO, "AMACO Brent Product Updates", https://amaco.com/amaco-brent-updates
**Confidence:** Medium
**Verification:** Both pages opened.
**Notes:** No manufacturer page states the full "under- or over-fired glaze will not look like the sample and may not be durable" claim in one place, so the guide does not make it. The only explicit under-firing statement found was a retailer blog, which is not cited.

### How manufacturer sample tiles are made
**Statement:** Mayco Stoneware tiles are white stoneware, slip cast, fired flat to cone 6 in oxidation. AMACO Potter's Choice tiles are AMACO 46-M buff stoneware fired to cone 5.
**Source:** Mayco, *Mayco Stoneware Glazes + Engobes User Guide* (2026 PDF), https://www.maycocolors.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/UsingMaycoStonewareGlazes.pdf ; Mayco Stoneware fired-colour page, https://www.maycocolors.com/color/fired/stoneware/ ; AMACO Potter's Choice brochure, https://amaco.com/asset/68caa3be24963
**Confidence:** High
**Verification:** PDFs and page opened.
**Notes:** AMACO's tiles are cone 5, not cone 6. The AMACO brochure does not state a coat count for the tile photos and does not use the word "oxidation" for them, so the guide attributes "oxidation" to Mayco only.

### Recommended brush coats
**Statement:** AMACO Potter's Choice: 3 coats unless the label notes otherwise. Mayco Stoneware: 2–4 coats depending on application weight; 2 on slip cast and 3 on handbuilt or wheel-thrown to resemble Mayco's tiles. Coyote: most glazes need 2 to 3 coats when brushing.
**Source:** American Art Clay Co., *Potter's Choice Glaze Application Notes* (rev. 6/13), https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/1015/2867/files/pc-glaze-application-brochure-web.pdf ; Mayco stoneware guide (above) ; Coyote, *Glazing and Firing Tips*, https://www.theceramicshop.com/downloads/Coyote%20glazing%20tips.pdf
**Confidence:** High
**Verification:** All three PDFs opened.
**Notes:** The AMACO and Coyote PDFs are retailer-hosted copies. AMACO Celadon coat counts were not verified and are not in the guide.

### What "food safe" and "dinnerware safe" mean
**Statement:** AMACO uses "food safe" for a finished, fired piece that does not release lead and cadmium above legal limits, and treats "dinnerware safe" as a separate durability question the customer must test. Mayco says finished-ware producers are responsible for food-contact testing, has moved to "suitable for dinnerware" wording on its website, and says it cannot test every glaze combination and so cannot certify combinations as food safe.
**Source:** AMACO product updates page (above) ; Mayco dinnerware page (above) ; Mayco stoneware guide (above)
**Confidence:** High for Mayco, Medium for AMACO
**Verification:** Pages and PDF opened.
**Notes:** No AMACO statement about layering was found; the layering caveat in the guide is cited to Mayco only. Mayco's 2026 guide PDF still uses "Food Safe" and "Dinnerware Safe" even though the website has changed terms.

### AP Non-Toxic seal is not a food-safety statement
**Statement:** The ACMI AP seal certifies that a product contains no materials in sufficient quantities to be toxic or injurious to humans. It concerns the art material, not fired ware.
**Source:** ACMI, "Classroom Art Materials Safety", https://www.acmiart.org/classroom-art-materials-safety ; Tony Hansen, "Food Safe", Digitalfire, https://digitalfire.com/glossary/food+safe
**Confidence:** Medium
**Verification:** Both pages opened.
**Notes:** The ACMI page never mentions food or fired ware; that the seal does not cover fired-glaze leaching is stated explicitly by Digitalfire, and the guide attributes it that way.

### FDA regulates leachable lead and cadmium
**Statement:** The FDA has compliance policy guides for lead and for cadmium contamination in ceramic foodware.
**Source:** FDA CPG Sec 545.450 (lead) and CPG Sec 545.400 (cadmium), fda.gov (full URLs in the guide's reference list)
**Confidence:** High
**Verification:** Both pages opened.
**Notes:** The numeric action levels did not come through in the fetched text, so no figures are quoted.

### Matte surfaces and cutlery marking; liner glazes
**Statement:** Cutlery marking usually happens when the surface is not smooth, as with mattes. For functional ware a stable transparent or white liner glaze is recommended.
**Source:** Tony Hansen, "Cutlery Marking", https://digitalfire.com/glossary/cutlery+marking ; "Liner Glaze", https://digitalfire.com/glossary/liner+glaze
**Confidence:** High
**Verification:** Both pages opened.
**Notes:** Staining of matte surfaces is only implied by the source, so the guide mentions cutlery marking only.

### Stable vs. fluid glazes
**Statement:** Mayco rates Stoneware glazes "Stable" or "Fluid" (fluid glazes often flow on vertical surfaces). AMACO says Blue Rutile and Albany Slip Brown can be very fluid if applied too heavily and advises feathering Palladium near the bottom of a piece. Coyote says to always leave room for the glaze to flow without sticking to the kiln shelf.
**Source:** Mayco stoneware guide ; AMACO Potter's Choice application notes ; Coyote glazing and firing tips (all above)
**Confidence:** High
**Verification:** PDFs opened.
**Notes:** Mayco's term is "Fluid". No manufacturer guidance on catch plates was found, so the guide does not mention them.

### Crazing and testing on your own clay
**Statement:** Crazing is a mismatch of thermal expansion between body and glaze. Mayco and Coyote recommend testing a glaze on the clay body you use.
**Source:** Tony Hansen, "Glaze Crazing", https://digitalfire.com/glossary/glaze+crazing ; Mayco Stoneware page (above) ; Coyote guidance reproduced at https://soundingstone.com/pages/coyote-glazes-brushing-drying-firing-guide
**Confidence:** High for the definition, Medium for the Coyote wording
**Verification:** Pages opened.
**Notes:** coyoteclay.com/Tips.html returned 404, so the Coyote wording is second-hand from a retailer page. No opened page mentions testing "in your own kiln" specifically, and no AMACO test-tile recommendation was confirmed.

### Clear glaze and underglaze
**Statement:** Underglazes are heavily pigmented, normally brushed onto leather-hard clay or greenware and covered with a transparent glaze. AMACO Velvet underglazes can be used with or without glaze and are food safe with a proper glaze. AMACO recommends 1–2 even coats of clear to prevent milky spots; Coyote says its clear can get milky if applied too thickly.
**Source:** Tony Hansen, "Underglaze", https://digitalfire.com/glossary/underglaze ; AMACO Velvet Underglaze, https://shop.amaco.com/glazes-underglazes/underglazes/v-velvet-underglaze ; AMACO, "Choosing A Clear Glaze", https://www.amaco.com/resources/blog/choosing-a-clear-glaze ; Coyote glazing and firing tips (above)
**Confidence:** High
**Verification:** Pages and PDF opened.
**Notes:** The AMACO clear-glaze article says nothing about dark clay or thick application over underglaze, so the guide does not claim it.
